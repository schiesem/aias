Describing an AI application in an automated plant means describing three things at once: the technical process the plant carries out, the devices that carry it out, and the AI that observes or steers it. The three are usually modelled in separate languages, and a statement that crosses from one to another, such as which device runs an inference and which process step that device also performs, cannot be made.

This pattern covers the first of the three. It formalises VDI/VDE 3682, *Formalised Process Description*, which is a graphical notation used in process and production engineering to describe what a plant does independently of how it is built. The notation has eight symbols and is deliberately small, which is what makes it a good basis for an ontology: a process is a network of states and the operators that transform them, and everything else is an attribute of those two.

The pattern is one of three subdomain patterns of the AIAS information model. The other two cover communication (ISO/IEC 7498-1) and artificial intelligence (ISO/IEC 22989). None of them is meant to be used on its own. They are imported by the AIAS alignment ontology, which ties them together at the points where the subdomains meet, and it is that alignment that lets a single question reach across all of them.

Two design decisions shape what this pattern looks like. Flows and assignments are edges in the graphical notation, and here they are classes. Reifying them this way costs a step of indirection when reading a model, and it buys the ability to say something about the connection itself rather than only about what it connects.
